Gastroenterology

Conditions Commonly Evaluated

Gastroenterology covers diseases of the entire gastrointestinal tract — from the oesophagus and stomach to the small intestine, colon and rectum.

A digestive symptom can have several possible causes. The same symptom may be simple in one patient and need more detailed evaluation in another.

  • GERD & acid reflux
  • Heartburn
  • Dyspepsia & indigestion
  • Gastritis
  • Peptic ulcer disease
  • Abdominal pain
  • Bloating & excessive gas
  • Irritable bowel syndrome
  • Chronic diarrhoea
  • Constipation
  • Incomplete evacuation
  • Altered bowel habits
  • Gastrointestinal bleeding
  • Ulcerative colitis
  • Crohn’s disease
  • Other intestinal disorders

Hepatology — Liver Care

Your liver can be unwell without telling you.

Liver disease can remain silent, particularly in its early stages. An abnormal liver-function test, fatty liver on ultrasound or an incidental finding may sometimes be the first indication that further evaluation is needed.

Fatty liver / metabolic liver diseaseHepatitis A, B, C & EAcute viral hepatitisAbnormal liver testsJaundiceChronic liver diseaseCirrhosisAscitesComplications of chronic liver disease

A normal-feeling liver does not necessarily mean a normal liver.

Gallbladder, Bile Duct & Pancreas

Small organs. Important problems.

These organs play important roles in digestion. Disorders involving them may produce overlapping symptoms and may need blood tests, ultrasound, CT/MRI or endoscopic evaluation to establish the diagnosis.

  • Gallstones
  • Common bile-duct stones
  • Biliary obstruction
  • Pancreatitis & pancreatic disorders
  • Other hepatobiliary & pancreatic conditions

Gastrointestinal Endoscopy

Seeing the problem helps us understand the problem.

  • Upper GI Endoscopy (OGD)Oesophagus, stomach and duodenum — for persistent reflux, upper abdominal symptoms, difficulty swallowing, vomiting or bleeding.
  • ColonoscopyFor GI bleeding, persistent change in bowel habits, unexplained anaemia, chronic diarrhoea, IBD, and appropriate screening or surveillance.
  • SigmoidoscopyA focused examination of the lower large intestine in selected situations.
  • ERCPRemoval of bile-duct and pancreatic stones; stenting for selected strictures.

When Should You See a Gastroenterologist?

Not every symptom is “just gas.”

These symptoms are common and often caused by simple conditions. When they persist, recur, worsen or come with warning signs, evaluation helps find the cause.

Acidity & Heartburn

Persistent heartburn, reflux, regurgitation or recurrent indigestion.

Abdominal Pain

Recurrent or unexplained pain, particularly when it persists or changes over time.

Bloating & Gas

Persistent bloating, belching, abdominal fullness or troublesome gas.

Constipation

Persistent constipation, difficult stools or a feeling of incomplete evacuation.

Diarrhoea

Recurrent or chronic loose stools, urgency or altered bowel habits.

Bleeding

Blood in the stool, black stools or unexplained anaemia.

Difficulty Swallowing

Difficulty or discomfort while swallowing food or liquids.

Appetite / Weight Loss

Persistent loss of appetite or unexplained weight loss.

Jaundice / Abnormal LFTs

Yellowing of the eyes or skin, raised liver enzymes or an unexplained abnormal liver report.

Approach to Patient Care

Medicine begins with listening.

Understand the problem → explain it clearly → choose proper treatment → follow up when needed.

  1. 01

    Listen

    Symptoms, their duration, pattern and effect on everyday life.

  2. 02

    Evaluate

    Medical history, examination findings and previous reports.

  3. 03

    Investigate

    Blood tests, imaging or endoscopy — when clinically indicated.

  4. 04

    Explain

    The diagnosis, findings and treatment options.

  5. 05

    Treat

    According to diagnosis, severity and your circumstances.

  6. 06

    Follow Up

    Monitor response and adjust the plan.

Good medicine is not always about doing more tests.
It is about doing the right things for the right patient at the right time.
